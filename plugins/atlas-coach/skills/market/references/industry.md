# The industry rules note

Load under `--industry` or `--gap`. Produces and maintains `docs/business/industry.md`: what this
industry expects, what it is legally required to do, and the patterns it has converged on. It is
the note that answers "is this normal here?" — and, when we find a gap, "how has this already been
solved?"

Distinct from `/market`'s default mode. Competitors are *who*; this is *the rules of the game they
all play by*. A competitor analysis goes stale in weeks; industry rules move in years, which is why
this note is refreshed rarely and cited often.

## Boundary, stated first

**This note is not legal, accounting, or medical advice, and it must say so in its own text.**
Regulatory rows carry a `⚠ verify` marker meaning: a professional in that field must confirm this
before anyone relies on it. A regulation summarized by a language model from secondary sources is a
starting point for a conversation with counsel, never a substitute for one. Getting this wrong is
not a documentation bug — it is the kind of mistake that costs a company money.

Never claim compliance. The note records what the industry is *required to consider*; whether this
project complies is a question for someone qualified to answer it.

## Rule families to cover

Not all apply to every industry. Ask which matter before researching all six.

| Family | What it captures |
|---|---|
| **Regulatory** | what the law requires — data protection, payment handling, accessibility, sector-specific statutes |
| **Standards** | formal interoperability specs the industry actually uses |
| **Conventions** | unlegislated user expectations so universal that violating them reads as broken |
| **Commercial** | pricing norms, billing cycles, refund windows, trial lengths |
| **Operational** | uptime expectations, support hours, data retention and export duties |
| **Anti-patterns** | things the industry tried and abandoned, and why — the cheapest lessons available |

The **conventions** and **anti-patterns** rows are the two most often missing from a project's
documentation and the two that most often explain a support ticket.

## Starter map

A prompt for the research, not an answer. Confirm the industry first — from
`docs/business/overview.md` if `/strategy-coach:blueprint` has run — then research what actually
applies, with dates. Never copy this table into the note as findings.

| Industry | Likely rule families to chase |
|---|---|
| **E-commerce** | card-payment handling, consumer distance-selling and returns rights, tax at point of sale, chargeback windows and evidence duties, abandoned-cart and email-consent norms, stock-oversell handling |
| **E-learning** | course-content interoperability standards, accessibility conformance, minor and student-record protections, certification and credit-hour rules, completion-tracking and assessment-integrity conventions |
| **Fintech / payments** | licensing and money-transmission, know-your-customer and anti-money-laundering duties, strong customer authentication, ledger immutability and audit trails, dispute timelines |
| **Healthcare** | patient-data protection, records retention and portability, clinical-safety classification, consent and audit requirements |
| **SaaS / B2B** | data-processing agreements and sub-processor disclosure, security attestation expectations, uptime commitments and credits, data export and deletion on termination, seat-based billing norms |
| **Marketplaces** | seller onboarding and verification, payout timing and escrow, liability split, dispute resolution, platform-transparency duties |

Each cell is a *search direction*. Turn it into named, dated instruments in the note — a rule with
no name and no date is not a rule, it is a rumour about a rule.

The **We** column is what makes this note load-bearing rather than educational: `file:line`,
`INFERRED`, or `NOT IN CODE`. A `NOT IN CODE` beside a regulatory row is the most important cell
this plugin can produce.

## `--gap "<description>"` — how has this been solved?

Given a gap, in this order:

1. **Restate it precisely**, and say which family it falls in. A gap you cannot state in one
   sentence is two gaps.
2. **Check what we already know** — `docs/business/industry.md`, `ENGINE search`, `docs/00-index.md`.
   The answer may already be written down.
3. **Research how the industry solved it.** Prefer: standards documents, published postmortems,
   open-source implementations you can read, and RFCs or specs — over blog posts describing them.
4. **Classify the answer honestly.** This is the whole value of the mode:

   | Verdict | Means |
   |---|---|
   | **Solved — converged** | one dominant approach; deviating needs a reason |
   | **Solved — divergent** | two or three legitimate approaches with different trade-offs; report the trade-off, not a winner |
   | **Solved — regulated** | the answer is dictated; ⚠ verify and stop guessing |
   | **Unsolved** | the industry has no accepted answer. Say so plainly |
   | **Dissolved** | the problem is avoided upstream rather than solved — usually the best answer available |

5. **Only then propose.** A proposal offered where precedent exists is negligence; a proposal
   offered where none exists is the job. Label it: **`NO PRECEDENT FOUND — proposal`**, with the
   trade-off you are accepting and the smallest thing that would prove it wrong.
6. **Write it back** into `industry.md` under the matching family, so the next gap search finds the
   answer instead of re-running. Then hand off: a gap that becomes work goes to
   `/strategy-coach:feature`, which turns it into a spec with a definition of done.

## Rules

- **Precedent before proposal.** Always search first. "Here is how three projects solved this" is
  worth more than any suggestion, and it is what was asked for.
- **⚠ verify on every regulatory row, no exceptions**, and never state that we comply.
- **Name the instrument and date it.** "GDPR-ish rules about data" is not a row.
- **"Unsolved" and "dissolved" are real answers** and often the most useful ones.
- **An anti-pattern we are currently doing is a finding**, and belongs in the report, not just the
  table.
