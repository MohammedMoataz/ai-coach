# Requirement formats

Load before writing stories or criteria. The value of a fixed form is not tidiness — it is that a
missing field becomes visible. A story with an empty `so that` is a story nobody can prioritize.

## The document

```markdown
> Generated by /analysis-coach:elicit · YYYY-MM-DD

# <What is being asked for>

## What this is for
One paragraph: the outcome, in the words of whoever asked. Quoted and attributed.

## Stakeholders and what each one decides
| Role | The decision only they can make | Asked? |
|---|---|---|
| billing lead | whether a partial refund may exceed the original charge | yes — 2026-08-27 |
| support lead | what the customer sees when it fails | not yet |

## Stories
### S1 — <short name>
As a <role>, I want <capability>, so that <outcome>.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **Given** an order with two shipments **When** one is refunded **Then** the order status reads
  `partially-refunded` and the remaining balance is the sum of the unshipped lines.
- **Given** a refund larger than the original charge **When** it is submitted **Then** it is
  rejected with `refund_exceeds_charge` and nothing is written.

## Assumptions
- `ASSUMED — billing lead to confirm`: refunds never cross currency.

## Open questions
| Question | Who owns it | Blocks |
|---|---|---|
| What happens to loyalty points on a partial refund? | loyalty PM | S1 |
```

## Acceptance criteria

Given/When/Then, one behaviour per criterion. The parts are load-bearing: **Given** is the state
that must already be true, **When** is the single triggering action, **Then** is what someone can
observe afterwards.

**Where this comes from, since it is often cited as if it were a standard.** Given/When/Then is
Gherkin — the BDD notation from Dan North's work around 2003, popularized through Cucumber
([cucumber.io/docs/bdd/history](https://cucumber.io/docs/bdd/history/), read 2026-08-27). It is
*not* an IIBA or BABOK standard, and North has said the notation is not the essence of BDD;
focusing on behaviour is. The checklist style is the older practitioner convention and has no
standards body behind it either. So: use this form because a missing field is visible in it, not
because a standard requires it — and if a team already writes checklists well, that is not a
defect to correct.

Three tests before a criterion is finished:

1. **Could someone else run it without asking you anything?** If not, it is a preference.
2. **Does it name an observable?** A value, a state, a message, a status code, a row in a table.
   "Works correctly" and "is fast" name nothing.
3. **Can it fail?** A criterion nothing could violate is a description, and describing the happy
   path is how the unhappy path ships unspecified. At least one criterion per story should be a
   rejection, an error or a boundary.

A criterion that fails any of the three is not deleted — it moves to **Open questions** with the
person who has to settle it, because an untestable requirement is usually an undecided one wearing
a testable shape.

## Stories

`As a <role>, I want <capability>, so that <outcome>.`

- **One outcome per story.** "And also" splits into two, always.
- **The role is a role, never "the user".** "As a warehouse picker" and "as a returns clerk" want
  opposite things from the same screen, and a story that says "user" hides which one lost.
- **The `so that` is the field that survives.** Wording runs out during implementation; the stated
  outcome is what lets someone make the right call at that point without asking.
- Size it so its criteria fit on one screen. A story with fifteen criteria is an epic that has not
  admitted it.

## "Ready" is not a standard; "done" is

Worth knowing before quoting either at a team. The Scrum Guide defines a **Definition of Done** as
one of Scrum's three commitments. It does **not** define a Definition of Ready — that is a
complementary practice teams invent for themselves
([scrum.org](https://www.scrum.org/resources/blog/why-isnt-definition-ready-described-scrum-guide),
read 2026-08-27), and no IIBA-recognized equivalent was found. Treat a team's DoR as a local
agreement to be surfaced and written down, never as a rule they are failing to follow.

## What this is not

This is elicitation output — what is wanted, and what would prove it was delivered. It stops before
the design. No table schemas, no endpoints, no component names: those belong in
`/strategy-coach:feature`'s spec, which reads this document as its input. Deciding the shape of the
solution while still eliciting is how a requirement quietly becomes a description of one
implementation.
